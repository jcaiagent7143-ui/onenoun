# Market entry review

Always pull the last four fiscal years from the filings before you start.

When you size the opportunity, work out the whole spend of everyone who could
conceivably buy, then narrow it to the part your product can actually serve,
then narrow again to the slice you could realistically win in three years.
Report all three, never just the largest.

Look at the structural forces around the industry before you look at any single
competitor: how much power the suppliers hold, how much the buyers hold, how
easy it is for a new entrant to appear, whether something outside the category
can replace the product entirely, and how hard the existing players fight each
other. A market can be growing fast and still be a bad market on these grounds.

Lay the company's internal advantages and weaknesses next to the external
openings and dangers in the same view, so the four quadrants can be read
against each other rather than as four separate lists.

Rank every initiative by how many customers it reaches, how much it moves the
needle for each of them, how sure you are of that estimate, and how much
engineering time it will take; then order by the first three multiplied
together and divided by the last.

Write each objective so that it names a specific outcome, a number that proves
it, someone who owns it, a reason it matters now, and the date it is due by.
An objective without all five is not an objective.

Remember that a team shipping a product will reproduce its own communication
structure in that product's architecture, so look at the org chart when the
integration plan looks strange.

Escalate to the committee chair if the deal exceeds the mandate.
